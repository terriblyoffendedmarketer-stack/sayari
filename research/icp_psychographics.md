## 1. Who they are 🟡
- **The compliance / sanctions / KYB officer:** accountable for calls they can't fully verify. Defends decisions to auditors and the board. Brief avatar: the Protector + the Skeptic.
- **The investigations / intelligence analyst:** traces ownership chains and builds cases. Proud of the craft, blocked by tools. Brief avatar: the Optimizer + the Builder.
- Often early-to-mid career at L1/L2 (alert review), with a few senior leads. Many came in through banking ops, law, or a CAMS-type certification. ✅ (listener: certification posts, "how do I break in", L1 talk)

## 2. What they feel at work ✅
- Powerless but accountable ("my name is on the file if I guess wrong")
- Undervalued: "a cost centre", "only noticed when it hits the fan" (r/FinancialCareers, r/AMLCompliance)
- Understaffed: "third analyst gone in 4 months", juniors leaving for 20–30% more pay (r/AMLCompliance, 58 upvotes)
- Sceptical of vendors: "ban vendor posts masquerading as practitioners" (38 upvotes); "AI is reducing alerts by 95%… is this just noise?"
- Quiet pride: "I loved doing it, this is where I was catching the real stuff" (transaction monitoring)

## 3. How they talk 🟡 → see `research/lexicon.md`
Dry, procedural, insider shorthand (L1, disposition, SAR). Humour is dark and self-deprecating, about being the "department of no".

# Lessons (the learning log; newest at the bottom)

## Lesson 1 · A day in the alert queue (2026-11-10)
**The job in one line:** a computer flags possible matches against sanctions lists all day; a human decides which ones are real and has to write down *why*.

**The loop an L1 analyst runs, maybe 30–100 times a day:**
1. **Alert lands:** "Possible match: *Jose Gomez* vs a sanctioned *José Gómez*." (The tool matches spellings, not people.)
2. **Compare the clues:** date of birth, country, address, company number. Often missing, which is the pain behind idea I06 (every Jose Gomez).
3. **Go hunting:** company registries, Google, news search, the spreadsheet. (The "swivel chair", P02's bingo.)
4. **Decide:** *false positive* → "clear" it, or *real or unsure* → escalate to L2 (a senior).
5. **Write the reason:** the part that matters months later when QA or an auditor asks "how did you decide this?" (P02's square, pillar 2).
6. **Repeat, against a daily target.** Most alerts turn out to be nothing (vendors claim 90–95%), but missing the one real hit is the career risk.

**Why this explains the psychology:**
- **Boredom plus fear at once:** 99 dull alerts, and the 100th could end your career. That's the "powerless but accountable" feeling in the brief.
- **Invisible when right, blamed when wrong:** "only noticed when it hits the fan", "a cost centre".
- **Pride in the catch:** "this is where I was catching the real stuff". The craft is the hook for the analyst persona.
- **High churn:** "third analyst gone in 4 months" (58 upvotes); juniors leave for fintech pay. Many readers are 1–3 years in, so career content may travel.

**Where Sayari fits in the loop:** steps 2–3 (the clues and the hunting: one entity, sources attached) and step 5 (the reason comes with evidence).
