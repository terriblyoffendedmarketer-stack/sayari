# Global Auto Safety UFLPA TPRM

Published: 2026-05-05
Type: case-study (anonymized)

## Leading European Automotive Group

An API-first UFLPA screening pipeline processing thousands of suppliers per cycle; the model was adopted across the broader automotive group.

## The challenge

UFLPA enforcement has expanded beyond consumer goods and apparel into the industrial supply chain. A single truck contains thousands of components sourced from suppliers in dozens of countries.

The commercial vehicle division of Europe's largest automotive group had no automated UFLPA screening capability. Like many manufacturers, the compliance team relied on manual processes and supplier self-attestation to manage forced labor risk. As CBP began targeting industrial components, the team recognized manual methods could not deliver the screening volume, speed, or sub-tier visibility required.

- No automated UFLPA screening: manual processes and supplier self-attestation only
- A single commercial vehicle contains thousands of components from dozens of countries
- Manual methods could not deliver volume, speed, or sub-tier visibility required
- EU Corporate Sustainability Due Diligence Directive will extend forced-labor regulations to Europe

## The solution

The division built an API-first screening pipeline, integrating entity resolution and supply chain mapping into procurement and compliance workflows, with batch screening of thousands of entities per cycle. The implementation was intensive: the original per-entity pricing model could not accommodate the volume (pricing was restructured around actual usage), and custom engineering meshed entity resolution output with internal risk scoring and workflow systems.

## Results

Suppliers are screened automatically and risks flagged programmatically. When European forced labor regulations arrive, the pipeline will be ready; the broader group will replicate a proven approach.
- 1000s of suppliers per batch cycle · API-first pipeline · reference implementation across the group

> "When European forced labor regulations arrive, the division's pipeline will be ready. The broader group will not need to build from zero." (Compliance Director, Leading European Automotive Group)
