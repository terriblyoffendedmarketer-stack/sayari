## The challenge: forced labor compliance built on supplier questionnaires

Consumer goods companies have become primary enforcement targets for forced labor regulations. Grocery retailers face UFLPA detention risk on any import with upstream connections to the Xinjiang region. CBP has detained shipments valued at nearly $3.7 billion under UFLPA enforcement since the law took effect.

One of the world's largest grocery retailers was managing its forced labor compliance through supplier questionnaires and self-attestation. Industry benchmarks for first-tier supplier self-assessment surveys consistently report response rates well below 50%, and the retailer's program was no exception. Even cooperative suppliers typically lacked visibility into their own upstream supply chains. Without tools to map supply chains beyond tier one, the retailer's compliance program was only as good as its suppliers' self-knowledge.

- Forced labor compliance built on supplier questionnaires and self-attestation
- Industry benchmarks: tier-one self-assessment response rates well below 50%
- Even cooperative suppliers typically lack tier-2/3 visibility into their own chain
- "Trust but cannot verify" posture: compliance only as good as suppliers' self-knowledge

## The solution: supply chain mapping that traces commodity flows independently

The retailer deployed a supply chain mapping platform to illuminate upstream supplier relationships beyond the tier-one level. Trade data and corporate registry connections provided the independent verification layer that questionnaire-based approaches lacked: the ability to trace commodity flows, identify sub-tier suppliers, and screen every entity in the chain against forced labor indicators and UFLPA entity lists.

The graph surfaces non-obvious relationships: shared ownership between a tier-one supplier and a sanctioned entity, beneficial ownership connections to state-owned enterprises, and corporate structure changes that indicate jurisdictional risk shifts. These are the connections questionnaire-based approaches cannot detect.

## Results: from "trust but cannot verify" to data-driven illumination

Sub-tier suppliers that had never appeared in any questionnaire response are now identified through trade data analysis and corporate registry mapping. Every tier, from raw material to retail shelf, is mapped and monitored.

- <50%: tier-1 questionnaire response rate (industry benchmark)
- $3.7B: CBP UFLPA detentions since June 2022
- Multi-tier: visibility from raw material extraction to retail shelf

> "Every tier, from raw material to retail shelf, is mapped and monitored. Independent verification across the entire supplier population." (Head of Forced Labor Compliance, Top 5 Global Grocery Retailer)
