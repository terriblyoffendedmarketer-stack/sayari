## Top 10 Global Electronics Manufacturer

From zero UFLPA capability to a documented, auditable compliance program in six months, across U.S. and Japan operations.
6 months from zero to documented UFLPA program · 2 operating divisions · Tier 1 → 5 supply chain visibility

## The challenge: no UFLPA program, no supply chain visibility, enforcement approaching
UFLPA created a rebuttable presumption that any goods connected to the Xinjiang region are made with forced labor and are barred from entering the US. For electronics manufacturers with hundreds of component suppliers and sub-tier intermediaries across Asia, supply chain compliance went from a reporting exercise to an import-blocking enforcement regime.

The company (a US business unit plus a Japan-based energy subsidiary) had no supply chain mapping tools, no supplier risk intelligence, no trade data access. Processes were entirely manual, with zero documentation to present to CBP if any shipment were questioned. The US team knew the exposure was real, but proving the absence of forced labor requires tracing supply chains multiple tiers deep.
- No mapping tools, no risk intelligence, no trade data: entirely manual
- Importing goods with potential upstream connections to Xinjiang-region entities
- Zero documentation capability if any shipment were questioned by CBP
- Internal friction: the Japan-based parent initially blocked the budget for the US team's evaluation

## The solution: full-stack supply chain intelligence built from zero
During early discovery, the team received a complimentary due diligence report on one of its own suppliers: a one-to-two-page analysis identifying specific UFLPA exposure to Xinjiang-connected entities. That moment shifted the conversation from "we might need a solution" to "we demonstrably have exposure and need one immediately."
Evaluation: pre-RFP workshops, a three-week trial of mapping and investigative tools, workflow automation presentations, and a second proof of concept for the Japanese team. The integrated Map + Graph + Guide architecture won over both teams.

## Results
The company can now respond to any CBP inquiry on any shipment, with tier 1 → tier 5 visibility through raw material extraction.

> "When the next UFLPA enforcement action targets the electronics sector, the company will be ready with evidence, not excuses." (Compliance Lead, Top 10 Global Electronics Manufacturer)
