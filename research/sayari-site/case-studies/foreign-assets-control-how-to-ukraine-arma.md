## How to Trace Hidden Foreign Assets of a Sanctioned Suspect

A sanctioned suspect re-registered assets to intermediaries in Ukraine to dodge sanctions and nationalization. ARMA had a domestic trail and a question no domestic record could answer: what did the suspects control abroad?

3 Foreign jurisdictions surfaced through Sayari · 4 Suspects linked to foreign entities · 2 Countries where foreign assets were confirmed

## The challenge: a domestic evasion scheme, an open question abroad

A Ukrainian law enforcement authority came to Asset Recovery & Management Agency (ARMA) with a request: trace the assets tied to a criminal investigation into document forgery and large-scale money laundering.

The case centered on individuals and foreign legal entities already under Ukrainian sanctions, including asset-blocking measures that prohibited the use or disposal of their property. Before those sanctions landed, one suspect (Suspect 1) held effective control over a group of Ukrainian companies. Under the law, the assets of those companies counted as assets controlled by a sanctioned person.

Then the trail went cold on purpose. Investigators suspected that, once sanctions took effect, Suspect 1 and several associates re-registered the companies' Ukrainian assets to intermediary entities, circumventing the sanctions and avoiding future nationalization. The same group was believed to have laundered assets of particularly large value.

Domestic tracing could follow the assets to the edge of the map. It could not follow them past it.

## How ARMA used Sayari to link the suspects to foreign companies

ARMA ran a comprehensive asset-tracing exercise inside Ukraine. In parallel, ARMA analysts opened the Sayari Platform to find the international links investigators did not yet know existed.

A Commercial World Model resolves records into entities, connects those entities across borders, and traces every finding back to a primary source. For an analyst chasing ownership through intermediaries, that connective tissue turns a dead end into a next step.

Working through Sayari, ARMA established 4 links that reopened the case:

- Czech Republic: Identified the corporate structure of a Czech company and established that Individual 1 was its founder.
- United Kingdom: Identified a residential address for Suspect 1 in the UK, giving investigators an additional jurisdiction for international enquiries.
- Slovakia: Uncovered the ownership structure of several Slovak companies and identified Suspects 2 and 3 as founders of these foreign companies. Identified that Suspect 4 holds a Slovak Individual Tax Number, tying a fourth suspect to the same jurisdiction.

Entities and cross-border ownership links reconstructed in the Sayari Platform. Solid connections were known prior to Sayari; dashed amber connections were discovered through the platform. Each resolves to a primary-source record.

## The results: turning the findings into formal international asset-tracing requests

The intelligence generated through Sayari was shared with the investigative authority immediately. ARMA submitted international asset-tracing requests to the competent authorities of Slovakia, the Czech Republic, and the United Kingdom.

Following the Sayari analysis, international cooperation confirmed assets held by the suspects in foreign jurisdictions: motor vehicles, a shareholding in a Slovak company, a land plot, real estate property, funds held in bank accounts.
