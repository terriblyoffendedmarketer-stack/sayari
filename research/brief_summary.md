# Sayari brief: the rules in one page

Source: "Sayari: Social Audit & Strategy" PDF (10 pages; the original is in the user's Downloads). Screenshots #01 and #02 in the idea doc are snippets of pages 2 and 10.

## Social mission (verbatim, p.2)
"We exist on social to stand up for compliance teams who are drowning. The ones manually screening in Excel, the analysts hitting dead ends, the ones losing sleep over whether they'll survive the audit."

## Our working deviation from the brief (agreed with the client, 2026-11)
The brief asks for memes ("Text-on-image or meme"). **For now Sayari doesn't want memes.** We make **branded content with personality** (realistic artifacts, typographic cards, charts, carousels in Sayari's design) and keep the brief's core idea: speak to the practitioner, not the buyer. Meme ideas are mined for their insight only.

## Strategy
- The category (Exiger, Altana, Kharon, Moody's) sells **fear, from authority, to executives**. Nobody talks to the practitioner. Sayari's whitespace: **human, validating, a little funny**, which competitors built on urgency can't follow without contradicting themselves.
- The website's precision ("judgment infrastructure", world-model stats) is **social kryptonite**: same asset, opposite effect. On social you have two seconds.

## Who we talk to
- **Primary:** compliance officers / heads of financial crime, sanctions, KYB (the Protector + the Skeptic: "I see you defending. Manual screening is killing you."); investigations & intelligence analysts (the Optimizer + the Builder: "Better signal. Faster hunting.").
- **Not optimized for:** supply chain leaders, CCO/CRO/CPO budget holders, government mission owners, AI/security/architecture approvers.
- **What they feel:** powerless but accountable, blamed for what they can't see, sceptical any tool is different, quietly terrified of the next audit.

## Voice
Validating · unflinching · human and a little funny · proof-backed. Banned words: see `research/lexicon.md`.

## The arc
Meet them mid-drown → name the real enemy (the Frankenstack, not them) → show the shift (one evidence-based system, the risk they couldn't see, an answer they can defend) → they go from defending to building.

## Pillars (each tied to a market truth in the brief)
1. **The Frankenstack Reckoning:** the work done by hand is what's drowning them. Provocation: "Your screening process is a spreadsheet, a Google tab, and a prayer." Brief's stats: 90% of execs admit siloed risk; 97% run overlapping vendors (**no source given; verify before using**).
2. **A Score Is Not a Defense:** when a regulator asks "how did you know?", only original-source evidence answers.
3. **The Risk Hiding in Plain Sight:** the exposure that ends careers is one ownership hop away, not on a list.

## Formats and mix (pp.7–10)
| Share | Format (brief's name) | Pillar | Brief's own example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 35% | "That's literally my job": pain mirror | Frankenstack | "when 'Global Electronics' triggers a sanctions match for the 5th time today" |
| 25% | "Wait, you can just do that?": product as relief | Frankenstack | 6 browser tabs to trace ownership → one Sayari query with the source document attached |
| 20% | "The receipts": investigation teardown | Hidden risk (+ score ≠ defense) | **The Moosavi / Hezbollah carousel:** a real designation mapped slide by slide, ending on "SDN screening is the floor, not the ceiling" ("one designation, five countries still exposed") |
| 20% | "Headline → what it means for you": newsjack | Score ≠ defense | a new OFAC action or UFLPA sector expansion → a two-line takeaway for screening teams |
- **Why front-load pain (35%):** the whole category leads with capability and threats. Pain earns attention; product earns its place as relief, never a brochure.
- **Receipts need a named analyst's face** on the carousel ("show, don't tell, what we caught"). It's the highest-performing format so far, per the brief.
- **The contrast is the strategy:** competitors converge on "polished, positive, instructional". Our content should look *jarringly different in-feed*.

## Channel and cadence
LinkedIn only, two surfaces: the **brand page** (pillars, proof points, newsjacks; 3–4×/week) and **ghostwritten exec / EGC posts** (Romu + leaders: first-person POV, hot takes, "behind-the-screen analyst moments"; 2–3×/week each). Newsjacks within 24–48h of a major regulatory or geopolitical event. Our scope so far: the brand page.
