# Practitioner lexicon: the words the ICP actually uses

Source: the "words practitioners use" screenshot in the idea doc (image #03, research notes with sources), the brief's banned words, and listener findings. Use it to make captions and mock images sound like the real job.
**The bar: hyper-relevant and relatable, not hyper-niche.** Only use terms that recur across many practitioners; prefer the most common phrasing.
**Beginner test:** "Safe" = a beginner in compliance gets it. "Explain" = define it in the same line if used.

## Words that name the pain (use these)
| Term | What it means in plain words | Use it for | Beginner | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Alert fatigue | Burnout from clearing endless alerts that turn out to be nothing | The daily grind | Safe | Sayari's own blog; common across the category |
| Swivel chair (swivel-chair effect) | Turning between disconnected tools and copy-pasting between them; **the real-world word for "Frankenstack"** and how analysts actually talk | The Frankenstack pillar | Safe | lyzr, finextra |
| Point solutions / silos / fragmented or disconnected systems | Tools that each do one job and don't talk to each other | Tool sprawl | Safe | regtechanalyst |
| Backlog / clearing alerts / "disposition" alerts | The queue piles up and the day goes on clearing it; "disposition" = deciding each alert's outcome | The queue | Safe ("disposition": explain) | jobgether |
| L1 / first-line review | The first analyst to look at an alert | Junior-analyst pain | Explain | jobgether |
| False positives | Alerts that look like a match but aren't. Vendors cite 90–95% in sanctions screening (**vendor-reported**; say so) | Name-match pain | Safe | sphinxhq |
| Spreadsheets and Google | Reviews run in Excel and manual web searches. A vendor survey: over a quarter manage periodic reviews mainly in spreadsheets; an older vendor study: 65% rely on Google for manual adverse-media searches (**vendor studies**; cite as such) | "A spreadsheet, a Google tab and a prayer" | Safe | regtechanalyst, prnewswire |
| Potential match | What the screening tool says when a name looks similar | P01's hook | Safe | listener, P01 |
| Clear by EOD | The deadline culture around alert queues | Pain mirror | Safe | idea bank |
| "How did you know?" | The examiner's question; the moment a score isn't enough | Score ≠ defense pillar | Safe | brief |

## Terms of the trade (fine to use, explain once)
| Term | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| Name matching | Comparing a name against a sanctions list (where false positives come from) |
| KYC refresh / periodic review | Re-checking a customer every 1–3 years |
| pKYC (perpetual KYC) | Checking continuously instead of every few years |
| UBO (ultimate beneficial owner) | The real person who ends up owning a company, through the layers |
| EDD (enhanced due diligence) | Extra checks for high-risk customers or suppliers |
| Audit trail | The record of why an alert was cleared or escalated |
| The 50% rule | A company 50%+ owned (together) by sanctioned people is itself sanctioned, even if its name isn't on the list |
| SDN list | OFAC's main sanctions list ("Specially Designated Nationals") |
| FOCI | Foreign ownership, control or influence (defense and telecom vendor checks) |
| UFLPA | US law that blocks imports linked to forced labor in Xinjiang |

## Words we never use (from the brief, plus vendor fluff)
judgment infrastructure · zero-resistance · leverage synergies · single pane of glass · holistic end-to-end · frictionless compliance · paradigm shift · best-in-breed seamless integration · next-gen risk ecosystem · world-model stats.
**Test:** if you wouldn't say it to a colleague over a beer, it doesn't go in a post.

## "Frankenstack": status
It's the brief's own word, so we use it, but practitioners say **"swivel chair"**, "silos" and "point solutions". Pair it with one of those the first time ("a Frankenstack: tools stitched together by a spreadsheet and one person's memory"), as P02 did.
