# Caption craft (company-agnostic)

Personality is the edge: most B2B accounts are drab and corporate. We **inform, relate, and have a voice**, all three in every caption. The client's specific voice lives in `content/VOICE.md`.

## Anatomy (LinkedIn)
1. **Hook (line 1, before "…see more").** It does the stopping. Patterns that work:
   - a quote the reader has heard ("Potential match." / "Quick one, can you double-check this?")
   - a precise, familiar moment ("It's 5:58 pm, and…")
   - a contrarian line ("A clean screen isn't a safe supplier.")
   - a number that surprises (sourced)
2. **The turn (2–4 short lines):** name the feeling, make it specific. Translate the jargon into what it means for *them*.
3. **The truth:** the insight, the rule, or the story's lesson. This is where the reader learns something.
4. **The brand, as relief:** one or two lines on what changes with the product, in plain words. Never a feature list.
5. **The close:** an easy reply prompt (count, tag, worst story), or a P.S. with a wink.

## Personality moves (use 1–2 per caption, not all)
- **Translation joke:** "Translation: that's your problem now."
- **Comic specificity:** "7 Google searches and a note you hope you can find later."
- **Deadpan understatement:** "It holds. Mostly."
- **The wink P.S.:** "P.S. if you're someone from Brightwell Components Ltd, our sincere apologies."
- **Taking their side:** "It's not you. It's the stack."

## Rules
- Short lines; white space; ~120–220 words; 0–3 emojis (usually 0).
- Words you'd say to a colleague over a beer. No vendor buzzwords.
- One idea per caption. If it needs two, it's a carousel.
- Every number is sourced (a link in the first comment if needed).
- Read it out loud: if it sounds like a brochure, rewrite the line.

## Before publishing: the caption checklist
☐ Line 1 stops the scroll on its own · ☐ a beginner follows it · ☐ one personality move lands · ☐ the brand appears as relief, not a pitch · ☐ the close invites an easy reply · ☐ sources noted
