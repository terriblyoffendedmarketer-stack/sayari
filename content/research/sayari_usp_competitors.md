Compiled 2026-11-09 from: Sayari's Social Audit & Strategy PDF (brief), sayari.com, competitor homepages (Exiger, Kharon), press coverage. Altana's homepage didn't render, so its entry relies on the brief and its tagline only.

## 1. What Sayari is, in one line

A commercial-risk intelligence company that maps who really owns and trades with whom, worldwide, from **primary-source documents**. Its products put that map in front of compliance and investigations teams with the **source record attached**.

Site positioning today: "AI that earns your trust", "We spent a decade mapping the world's shadow economy", and "Intelligence you can verify. Judgment that scales." (The brief says the "judgment infrastructure" language stays off social.)

## 4. The USP, plainly

1. **Source, not score.** Every answer links back to the original registry filing, court record, or customs document. Competitors mostly show an aggregated score or their own interpretation. This is Pillar 2, "a score is not a defense".
2. **Ownership depth.** Sayari walks the chain past the first owner, catching the 50%-rule exposure that name screening misses. This is Pillar 3, "the risk hiding in plain sight" (the 72% stat).
3. **Primary-source global coverage at scale.** 250+ jurisdictions built from original documents, including hard-to-reach registries. This is why the graph has things others don't.
4. **One place for record, source and decision** (Guide + Graph). This replaces the swivel chair of 4 to 7 tools. It's Pillar 1, "the Frankenstack reckoning".
5. **Tradecraft heritage.** Built with and for government investigators (Treasury, CBP, In-Q-Tel). It earns credibility with analysts, but use it sparingly on a practitioner-first page.

The one-liner for social: **"Your call comes with its evidence."** (Already the Post 1 sign-off, and it's the right anchor.)

## 5. Competitors: what they own, and what Sayari can say that they can't

| Competitor | What they own | Voice | Gap Sayari can take (from the brief + research) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Exiger** | "The AI partner for supply chain & procurement automation"; 1Exiger platform; Gartner MQ Leader (supplier risk) 2 years running; 150+ Fortune 500, 60+ federal agencies | Bold, urgent, exec-facing, analyst-badge-heavy | Black-box scores that can't explain **why**. Talks AT execs. No humour, no relief. |
| **Altana** | "The AI-powered network for trusted trade"; a supply-chain AI graph | Authoritative, relentless urgency, founder/press-led | Trade flows without ownership depth; **inferred** maps vs. Sayari's documented records. All doom. |
| **Kharon** | "The premier global risk analytics platform"; ClearView, GraphCast; 7 of the top 10 US banks; 35+ Fortune 100; forced-labour and military-end-use specialism; The Brief (analyst publication) | Confident, superlative ("No compromises."); dry analyst briefings | Strong on sanctions research. The brief notes they do **dry briefings**, so Sayari can turn the same tradecraft into stories with a named analyst's face. |
| **Moody's (Orbis / Maxsight)** | The corporate-data incumbent; a unified risk platform push | Measured, institutional, "decision-grade" | **Aggregated** data, not the original source. Shows interpretation, not the record. |
| **Name-screening incumbents** (LSEG World-Check, Dow Jones) | Watchlist / PEP screening | Corporate | Name lookups produce the false-positive grind (Jose Gomez, Brightwell). They answer "is the name on a list?", not "who owns it?". |
| **Supply-chain risk platforms** (Resilinc, Assent, Sourcemap, Prewave) | Disruption and ESG mapping | Ops / procurement | Supplier-reported data (questionnaires) vs. records. This is the "questionnaire vs record" idea. |
| **The real #1 competitor: doing it by hand** | Excel, Google, government registry sites, questionnaires, consultants | n/a | The Frankenstack. Most posts should be aimed here, not at named rivals. |

**Brief's strategic insight:** the whole category sells fear from authority to executives. Sayari wins on social by being the only **human, validating, a-little-funny** voice for the practitioner, and competitors built on urgency can't follow without contradicting their brand.

**Never name competitors in posts.** Contrast through the *practice* ("a score", "a questionnaire", "a name match"), not the brand.
