## The name passed, the owner didn't
Status: validated (Claude's call, 2026-11-10; the user delegated the pick)

**In plain words:** screening checks a company's *name* against a sanctions list. But the danger usually sits with *who owns* the company. So a clean result on the name can still hide a sanctioned owner, one step up the chain.

**The feeling:** "I did everything right and still got caught out." A false sense of safety, plus the fear of being blamed for something the tool never checked.

**Evidence (three kinds of source):**
- *Practitioners and founders (listener):* "Are you actually manually looking up each entity on the OFAC website?" (r/business); "You can and will be unbanked for doing business with sanctioned entities" (r/business, 33 upvotes). People treat the name search as the whole job.
- *Sayari case studies:* "Your vendors passed sanctions screening. Their owners were not checked." (#3 US telecom) · a live sanctioned vendor caught on an energy company's *active* vendor list through an ownership chain the legacy tool missed · "a sanctioned entity operating through a subsidiary would pass the screen undetected" (Fortune 100 manufacturer) · "OFAC 50 Percent Rule exposure: ownership-based restrictions invisible to list screening" (energy trader) · "The shipment cleared the customs declaration. The consignee's owner did not." (logistics)
- *Enforcement:* PURE, OFAC settlement, $466,200 (2023). The owner was named in their own files and the name search kept coming back clean.

**Why it's Sayari's to say:** ownership depth is the core USP (*who owns the owner*), and source-linked records answer "how did you know?".

**Posts it can carry:** idea 4 ("No match found" is the start of the question) · idea 5 (questionnaire vs record) · the "live sanctioned vendor" story as a receipts carousel · the iceberg template (on the list vs one hop away).
