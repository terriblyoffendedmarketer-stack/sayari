## Concepts
**A. "It won't arrive as crypto" (GIF)** ← recommended
- Frame 1: a stream of coins/tokens moving along a chain line, with a big number: $6.3bn.
- Middle: as the stream leaves the chain, each coin turns into a company card (a trading company, a supplier, a consultancy; invented names).
- End: the cards drop into a realistic **"Onboarding queue"** (native UI look, white rows). The last row is highlighted in gold: "Sanctions name screen ✓ No match", plus a question chip: "Who's behind it?"
- Why: motion shows the one thing a still can't, the money changing form. And the end frame is the reader's own screen.
- Risk: has to stay simple (3 cards max, ~10 s loop).

**B. "Your queue, today" (GIF)**
- A realistic onboarding/payments queue fills with ordinary companies. One row expands to show its owners and controllers, and a hidden link appears.
- Why: pure pain mirror, with the variety-agenda native UI.
- Risk: the Binance link is only in the headline. Expanding to an ownership chain drifts toward Sayari's template.

**C. "Are we exposed?" (carousel, 5–6 slides)**
- Cover: "$6.3bn stolen. The question on your desk: are we exposed?" → where the money is (frozen vs still moving, one bar) → it has to pass through people and companies (onboarding / payments / supply chain) → a name match isn't the answer ("✓ No match" vs owners and controllers) → the four-tool hunt (search tool, registry, spreadsheet, an hour) → the close question.
- Why: saveable, explains more, follows the caption.
- Risk: repeats the caption, and a newsjack carousel is slower to make.

## Rethink (user, 2026-11-12): v2 doesn't work without the caption
**Problem:** "The Binance money has to land somewhere. It could be your queue." assumes the reader knows the story. It never says what happened, who did it, or why it's a sanctions problem. "Your queue" is vague, and the coin animation is decoration, not information.
**The image alone must say three things:** (1) what happened, in one line (who stole how much, from whom; FBI → North Korea), (2) why it reaches a compliance desk (billions still have to be cashed out through ordinary-looking companies), (3) the question it leaves them with.
**Message options (pick one before any format):**
- M1: "North Korea stole $6.3bn in crypto. To spend it, it needs companies that look like your customers." (+ FBI attribution line)
- M2: "$6.3bn stolen. About half frozen. The rest needs a way out, and the way out looks like an ordinary company."
- M3: "The biggest crypto theft ever just became a KYB problem."
Format follows the message: a GIF builds the one sentence in steps; a carousel gives each beat a slide.

## Caption fix: "Western agencies"
That's the BBC's wording, and it's vague. The concrete source: the **UN Panel of Experts (2024 report)** said cyber theft brings in about half of North Korea's foreign currency and funds about 40% of its weapons of mass destruction programmes. Suggested line: "A 2024 UN report found North Korea funds about 40% of its weapons programs through cyber theft." (The panel's mandate ended in 2024, so this is its last report.)

## Certainty check: "North Korea stole it" (user asked, 2026-11-12)
- **What's certain:** $6.3bn was taken from Binance without authorisation (Binance confirms).
- **Who did it is an attribution, not a proven fact.** The FBI publicly attributed it to North Korea's TraderTraitor unit, and Chainalysis, TRM Labs and Elliptic agree. No one has been charged, no court has ruled, Binance hasn't confirmed it, and North Korea denies these accusations. (FBI attributions like this, e.g. Bybit 2025, are rarely walked back, but they're still claims.)
- **Image rule:** never "North Korea stole…". Use "The FBI says North Korea…" or "FBI: North Korea behind…". Before posting, read the FBI notice itself (so far we've only seen press coverage of it).
- **Refined messages:**
  - M1: "The FBI says North Korea stole $6.3bn in crypto. To spend it, it needs companies that look like your customers."
  - M3: "The biggest crypto theft ever just became a KYB problem." (makes no claim about who), second beat: "The FBI says North Korea did it, and to spend the money, it needs companies that look like your customers."
