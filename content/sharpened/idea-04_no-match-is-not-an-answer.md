# Idea 4, sharpened: "No match found" is the start of the question

**Core truth:** a sanctions-list search checks a *name*. The 50% rule is about *owners*. A company 50%+ owned by sanctioned people is blocked even if its own name is on no list, and OFAC keeps no public list of those companies.

## The real story we found (verified on OFAC's site)

**PURE (an insurer), OFAC settlement, 21 Dec 2023, $466,200**
- In **2010**, a client questionnaire named **one shareholder** of a Panama company: Viktor Vekselberg.
- In **April 2018**, Vekselberg was sanctioned. From that day the company was blocked too, under the 50% rule, though its own name was on no list.
- The ownership info sat in PURE's files but never reached its screening system. The name kept coming back clean.
- **39 violations** followed over four insurance policies, May 2018 to July 2020.
- The aggravating point OFAC flagged: no review of screening gaps after the 50% rule was expanded in 2014.

Why it's a great story: nobody lied and nothing was hidden. **The answer was already in their own files.** That makes it relatable (it's a process gap, not villainy) and it leads straight to Sayari's "ownership with the source attached" without a hard sell.
